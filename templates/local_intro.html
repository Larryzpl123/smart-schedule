Local mode

The same schedule app, with no account and no server. Your schedule is kept in this browser's own storage. We never receive it, so we cannot lose it, read it, or hand it to anyone. Nobody has to wait for a verification email either.

The trade is real, so read the next bit before you type a term's worth of homework into it.
What you give up
  • It lives in one browser on one device. Your phone and your laptop are two different copies, and neither knows about the other.
  • Clearing your browsing data deletes it. So does "reset" on a shared or school-managed machine.
  • There is no password to recover and no version history, because there is nothing on our side to recover it from.
  • On iPhone and iPad, Safari deletes storage a site wrote with a script after 7 days with no visit. Adding it to your Home Screen is what turns that off. See below.
Install it as an app. On an iPhone this is not optional.
The rule that deletes your data is a Safari rule, not a web rule, so what you need to do depends on what you are using. The app works this out for itself and shows you the steps; here is the whole picture.
Where you open it What can delete your schedule What to do
iPhone or iPad, any browser A 7 day timer. Safari clears a site's stored data after a week of not opening it, and every browser on iOS is Safari underneath. Share, then Add to Home Screen. A Home Screen app is exempt.
Mac, Safari The same 7 day timer. File, Add to Dock (macOS Sonoma or newer).
Chrome, Edge, Samsung, Opera (not on iOS) No timer. Only a disk running low, and then least-visited sites first. Install it from the address bar or the menu, and press protect the storage in Settings.
Firefox No timer either. Firefox does not install websites as apps. Press protect the storage in Settings and say yes to the prompt.
On iPhone and iPad, install it before you type anything in. A Home Screen app gets its own storage, separate from the browser's, so a schedule entered in Safari first does not follow it across. If you have already entered one, use Backup to copy it out and paste it back in over there.
None of this survives you clearing your browsing data. That is what the backup is for.
Backing it up
The schedule page has a ⛁ Backup button. It will copy your schedule out as plain text (paste it into Notes, or a message to yourself) or download it as a .txt file, and it will read either one back in. That is also how you move to a new phone or a new browser. Local mode reminds you about this once a day; you can turn that off in its settings.
